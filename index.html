Physics & science education researcher

Tom Reshef-Israeli

PhD candidate, Faculty of Education in Science and Technology, Technion Israel Institute of Technology

I am a PhD candidate in physics and science education, advised by Shulamit Kapon. My research examines learners’ social and epistemic processes as they engage in modeling physical phenomena. This includes learners’ use of mathematics in physics learning, how uncertainty shapes learners’ collaborative reasoning, and how students frame laboratory inquiry.

With a background in physics and cognitive science and experience teaching physics and mathematics, I teach pre-service and in-service physics teachers as well as undergraduate physics students. I strive to connect research on learning with classroom and laboratory teaching.

Projects

I play a central role in the following research & development projects:

01

Mathematical modeling in physics learning

Designing modeling activities and investigating students’ pathways and teacher interventions, including how shared understanding develops and how learners respond to epistemic uncertainty.

02

Learner-level discovery through mathematics

Conceptualizing the physical insights learners can discover through mathematical structures, and designing and investigating classroom discovery activities.

Related publication: Discovery through math (Phys. Rev. Phys. Educ. Res.) ↗
03

Framing and learning in collaborative laboratory inquiry

Characterizing how undergraduate physics students frame collaborative laboratory inquiry and how their framing and learning interact with their instructor’s framing.

Related talk: Negotiating social and epistemic participation (NARST 2026) ↗
04

Curriculum for meaningful engagement in experimental practices

Developing curriculum and learning materials for secondary school physics laboratories that support meaningful engagement in experimental practices.
